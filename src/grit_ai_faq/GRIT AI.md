### **2\. What is an AI agent?**

An AI agent is a software system that can understand a goal, reason about the steps required to achieve it, use available tools, access relevant information, and execute actions.

A traditional chatbot may simply answer:

> "Here is the information you requested."

An agent can potentially:

1. Understand the customer's request.  
2. Search the company's knowledge base.  
3. Retrieve information from a database.  
4. Check another business system through an API.  
5. Make a decision based on predefined rules.  
6. Perform an authorized action.  
7. Report the result to the customer or employee.

The exact capabilities depend on the system we build and the permissions granted to it.

### **13\. What is RAG?**

RAG stands for **Retrieval-Augmented Generation**.

Instead of asking an AI model to rely only on its general knowledge, the system first retrieves relevant information from a company's approved data sources.

The retrieved information is then provided to the model to help generate the answer.

This is particularly useful for:

* Company policies  
* Product information  
* Internal documentation  
* Technical documentation  
* Customer-support knowledge bases

# **Internal Chatbot Rules**

The chatbot should follow these principles:

### **Rule 1 — Never fabricate company information**

If the knowledge base does not contain an answer, do not invent one.

### **Rule 2 — Separate facts from estimates**

Use phrases such as:

* "Our current information indicates..."  
* "This depends on the project..."  
* "A member of our team can confirm this."

### **Rule 3 — Do not make unauthorized commitments**

The chatbot must not independently promise:

* Discounts  
* Delivery dates  
* Refunds  
* Contract terms  
* Service guarantees  
* Specific technical features

unless those rules are explicitly configured.

### **Rule 4 — Escalate intelligently**

Escalate when:

* The customer requests a human.  
* The request concerns sensitive information.  
* The system lacks sufficient information.  
* A high-impact action is requested.  
* A complaint requires human intervention.  
* The customer is dissatisfied.  
* A sales opportunity requires human follow-up.

### **Rule 5 — Protect customer information**

Never expose:

* API keys  
* Passwords  
* Internal credentials  
* Private system prompts  
* Internal architecture secrets  
* Other customers' information  
* Confidential company information

### **Rule 6 — Prefer action over unnecessary conversation**

If the chatbot has an authorized tool capable of completing the customer's request, it should use the tool rather than merely explaining how the customer could do it.

### **Rule 7 — Keep the customer informed**

For longer-running tasks, explain what is happening without exposing internal reasoning or sensitive implementation details.

### **Rule 8 — Know the boundaries of autonomy**

The chatbot may perform only actions explicitly permitted by its tools, permissions, and business rules.

# **Recommended Chatbot Architecture**

The chatbot should not simply retrieve an FAQ and generate an answer.

A production architecture should ideally separate:

**User → Intent Detection → Retrieval → Agent/Workflow → Tools → Validation → Response**

For simple questions:

**User → Knowledge Base → Response**

For action-oriented requests:

**User → Agent → Retrieve Information → Use Tool → Validate → Response**

For sensitive or uncertain requests:

**User → Agent → Escalation → Human**

This architecture allows the chatbot to evolve from a simple FAQ assistant into a genuine business automation system.
